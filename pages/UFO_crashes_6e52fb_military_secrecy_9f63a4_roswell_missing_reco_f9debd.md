## Introduction

One of the most persistent features of the Roswell debate is not a witness statement or a piece of debris. It is the absence of [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}). In the 1990s, a federal investigation found that some key Roswell Army Air Field files that might have shed light on events in 1947 had been destroyed. That finding did not establish that an alien craft crashed near Roswell, nor did it uncover [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) of a secret recovery programme. What it did create was a “suspicion gap”: a space between what officials say happened and what surviving documentation can prove. When records are missing, even ordinary explanations become harder for some people to fully trust.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-95-187/html/GAOREPORTS-NSIAD-95-187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GAOREPORTS NSIAD</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947...Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Cra...</span></span></span>

## What the GAO Found Missing

In 1994, Congressman Steven Schiff asked the US General Accounting Office (now the Government Accountability Office, or GAO) to investigate government records relating to the 1947 Roswell incident. The resulting inquiry searched military, intelligence and archival holdings for documentation connected to the alleged crash.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947...GAO provided information on the 1947 weather balloon crash at Roswell Army Air...</span></span></span>

## The Two Surviving 1947 Records

Despite the missing files, the GAO did locate two contemporaneous government records that referred to the Roswell event. These documents are important because they were created in 1947 rather than decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashOur search for government records concerning the Roswell crash yielde...</span></span></span>

The first was a July 1947 history report prepared by the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field. According to the GAO, the report noted the recovery of a “flying disc” that military officials later determined was a radar-tracking balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashOur search for government records concerning the Roswell crash yielde...</span></span></span>

The second was an FBI teletype dated 8 July 1947. It recorded military information indicating that the recovered object resembled a high-altitude weather balloon equipped with a radar reflector. The FBI has preserved this message in its released files, making it one of the most frequently cited primary documents in discussions of Roswell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=ufo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">23 items matching your search termsOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembl...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Just as significant as what the GAO found was what it did not find. The investigation reviewed classified and unclassified records from multiple agencies and reported that no additional government documents describing a crashed spacecraft or recovered extraterrestrial [bodies]({{ 'bodies/' | relative_url }}) were located. Executive agencies responding to GAO inquiries likewise produced no further Roswell crash records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashOur search for government records concerning the Roswell crash yielde...</span></span></span>

## Why Thin Archives Invite Larger Stories

Roswell illustrates an important pattern in long-running historical controversies: incomplete archives often encourage speculation because they make definitive closure impossible.

If a complete set of 1947 records had survived and clearly documented the chain of events, the Roswell debate might look very different today. Instead, researchers confront a fragmented [paper trail]({{ 'paper-trail/' | relative_url }}). The surviving records support a balloon-related explanation, but the destruction of other files means that critics can argue that relevant evidence may have been lost. Supporters of the official explanation can point out that no surviving document supports an alien-recovery claim.
