## Introduction

One of the most significant findings in the Pentagon’s recent review of UFO crash-retrieval allegations was not about extraterrestrials at all. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), created to investigate [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) anomalous phenomena (UAP), concluded that some witnesses were pointing to real classified military and intelligence programmes—but were incorrectly interpreting them as [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) of alien technology. According to AARO, secrecy, compartmentalisation and limited access to sensitive information created conditions in which genuine national-security activities could be misunderstood and later retold as stories about recovered spacecraft or reverse-engineering programmes. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">media engagement with acting aaro director tim phillips on the historical recor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarMedia Engagement With Acting AARO Director Tim Phillips...6 Mar 2024 — AARO assesses that alleged hidden UAP programs e...</span></span></span>

Its conclusion was that confusion surrounding legitimate secret programmes is a key mechanism behind many enduring alien-retrieval narratives. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">media engagement with acting aaro director tim phillips on the historical recor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarMedia Engagement With Acting AARO Director Tim Phillips...6 Mar 2024 — AARO assesses that alleged hidden UAP programs e...</span></span></span>

## How Real Secrecy Can Feed Wrong Conclusions

Highly classified defence programmes are intentionally designed so that most personnel see only a small part of the overall activity. Even people with security clearances may know that a project exists without understanding its true purpose. AARO argued that this environment can encourage speculation when individuals encounter unusual equipment, restricted facilities, unexplained tests or heavily compartmentalised information. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">media engagement with acting aaro director tim phillips on the historical recor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarMedia Engagement With Acting AARO Director Tim Phillips...6 Mar 2024 — AARO assesses that alleged hidden UAP programs e...</span></span></span>

## What AARO Checked With Programme Overseers

AARO did not rely solely on witness accounts. Investigators reviewed classified and unclassified archives, examined historical records and interviewed officials responsible for overseeing sensitive defence and intelligence programmes. The office reported that it had access to programme managers, senior officials and organisations named by witnesses as allegedly possessing alien technology. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 18 Mar 2024 — Results: Project SAUCER did not find evidence of extraterrestria...</span></span></span>

One recurring allegation involved defence contractors supposedly studying recovered extraterrestrial materials. AARO reported that executives, scientists and chief technology officers from companies identified by interviewees met with investigators and denied possessing or reverse-engineering off-world technology. AARO stated that it found no evidence supporting those claims. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/84 May 2024 — &quot;Aliens&quot; Present During a DoD Technology Test: AARO reviewed...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

The office also examined allegations that hidden government programmes were operating outside congressional oversight. Its assessment was that alleged secret UAP programmes either did not exist or were actually authentic national-security programmes unrelated to extraterrestrial technology. In other words, investigators often found a real classified activity behind the rumour, but not an alien one. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/3702219/media-engagement-with-acting-aaro-director-tim-phillips-on-the-historical-recor/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">media engagement with acting aaro director tim phillips on the historical recor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarMedia Engagement With Acting AARO Director Tim Phillips...6 Mar 2024 — AARO assesses that alleged hidden UAP programs e...</span></span></span>

## A Concrete Example: Programmes That Acquired an Alien Reputation

AARO’s historical report described cases in which genuine classified efforts gradually acquired a UFO-related reputation. In some instances, intelligence or defence projects became associated with extraterrestrial narratives because participants knew only fragments of the programme and filled gaps with speculation.
