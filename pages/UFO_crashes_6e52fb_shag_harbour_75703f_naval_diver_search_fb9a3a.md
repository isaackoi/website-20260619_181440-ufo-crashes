## Introduction

The naval diver search at Shag Harbour is one of the most important pieces of the entire incident because it moved the case beyond witness testimony and into a direct attempt to recover [physical evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}). After reports that an unidentified object had descended into the water near the Nova Scotia fishing community on 4 October 1967, Canadian authorities treated the event as a possible aircraft crash. When [no missing aircraft]({{ 'no-missing-plane/' | relative_url }}) could be identified, the Royal Canadian Navy sent professional divers to investigate the reported impact area. Their conclusion was strikingly simple: they found no object, no wreckage, no debris field, and no bodies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

That negative result did not solve the mystery. Instead, it became one of the central reasons the Shag Harbour case remains debated. The underwater search examined the location where physical evidence should have existed if a conventional [aircraft]({{ 'aircraft/' | relative_url }}) had crashed, yet nothing conclusive was recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

## Why Divers Were Sent

The decision to deploy naval divers was not made because authorities believed a spacecraft had crashed. Initially, local witnesses, police officers, and rescue personnel thought an aircraft might have gone down in the water.

Search-and-rescue efforts began almost immediately. Fishing boats and a Canadian Coast Guard vessel searched the reported impact area looking for survivors or [wreckage]({{ 'wreckage/' | relative_url }}). By the following day, however, officials had established that no civilian or military aircraft were missing along the region's eastern seaboard. This removed the most obvious explanation while leaving the reported water impact unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

According to [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) associated with Canada's official UFO files, the incident was passed through military channels as a UFO report. Royal Canadian Navy headquarters then tasked Fleet Diving Unit Atlantic with conducting an underwater search of the seabed near the reported crash site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

The diver operation therefore represented a direct test of the crash claim. If something substantial had entered the water, investigators expected at least some physical trace.

## What the Underwater Search Reported

### The Search Area

Navy divers arrived roughly two days after the sighting and searched the seabed in the waters off Shag Harbour for several days. Fleet Diving Unit Atlantic conducted systematic underwater examinations of the area where witnesses and search crews believed the object had entered the sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

The operation was significant because it involved trained military divers rather than civilian observers. Their mission was not to interpret lights in the sky but to locate a physical object beneath the water.

### The Official Result

The reported outcome of the search was unequivocal. Divers found no trace of a crashed object. No aircraft wreckage was recovered. [No debris]({{ 'no-debris/' | relative_url }}) field was identified. No [bodies]({{ 'bodies/' | relative_url }}) were found. The final search report stated that no object could be located on the seafloor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

This finding is frequently overlooked in popular retellings of the case. The underwater operation did not produce a hidden craft, exotic material, or any recovered technology. From an evidential standpoint, the search was a negative result.

That does not mean nothing unusual occurred. It means that the strongest attempt to obtain physical confirmation failed to produce recoverable evidence. The distinction is important because many later discussions focus on what witnesses reported seeing rather than on what investigators actually recovered.

## Why Finding Nothing Matters

The diver search occupies a unique place in the Shag Harbour story because it cuts both ways.

For sceptics, the absence of wreckage weakens the idea that a large physical object crashed into the harbour. Aircraft accidents normally leave debris, fuel traces, structural fragments, or other recoverable evidence. A search that finds none of these raises the possibility that witnesses misinterpreted what they observed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

For proponents of the mystery, the negative result creates a different question. Multiple witnesses believed something entered the water, rescue personnel searched the location, and authorities considered the incident serious enough to warrant military involvement. If an impact occurred, why was nothing found? The lack of debris becomes part of the puzzle rather than a resolution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

The diver search therefore did not confirm a UFO crash, but neither did it provide a conventional explanation. Instead, it established one of the case's most enduring facts: the location where investigators expected physical evidence yielded none.

## The Lasting Significance of the Diver Search

Many UFO crash stories rely entirely on testimony or later recollections. Shag Harbour stands apart because authorities mounted a real underwater recovery effort. The involvement of Fleet Diving Unit Atlantic gave the investigation an unusual level of official scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>

Yet the most important outcome was not a discovery but an absence. After days of searching, naval divers reported no object on the seabed and recovered no evidence capable of identifying what witnesses believed had entered the water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shag_Harbour_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Shag Harbour UFO incident</span></span></span>
