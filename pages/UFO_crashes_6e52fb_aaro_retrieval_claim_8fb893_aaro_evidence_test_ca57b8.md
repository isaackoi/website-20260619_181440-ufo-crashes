## Introduction

When the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})) examined modern UFO crash-retrieval allegations, it did not treat them primarily as stories about unusual objects in the sky. It treated them as claims that a hidden institutional system existed: a long-running programme capable of recovering, storing, transporting, funding, protecting, and studying allegedly non-human technology. AARO’s central question was therefore not whether witnesses sincerely believed such a programme existed, but what [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) a real retrieval programme would leave behind. According to AARO’s 2024 historical review, investigators found no verifiable evidence that the US government or private industry possessed extraterrestrial technology, nor evidence of a hidden programme improperly concealed from Congress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 18 Mar 2024 — found no evidence that U.S. companies ever possessed off-world techno...</span></span></span>

## What Would Prove a Hidden Retrieval Program?

AARO's historical report approached retrieval allegations as claims about systems rather than isolated events. If recovered non-human technology had been secretly studied for decades, investigators argued that evidence should exist beyond witness recollections. Such a programme would require facilities, security approvals, transportation arrangements, scientific personnel, funding streams, [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) management, contractor participation, and legal authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 18 Mar 2024 — found no evidence that U.S. companies ever possessed off-world techno...</span></span></span>

The office therefore looked for indicators such as:

* Official records authorising or documenting retrieval activities.
* Special Access Program (SAP) or controlled-access programme documentation.
* Budget allocations and contracting records.
* Physical materials with documented custody chains.
* Scientific reports or technical analyses tied to recovered objects.
* Oversight records showing programme reporting requirements.
* Consistent testimony from individuals with direct, first-hand access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 18 Mar 2024 — found no evidence that U.S. companies ever possessed off-world techno...</span></span></span>

The underlying logic was straightforward: a genuine decades-long retrieval effort would have to interact repeatedly with government bureaucracy and industry. Even highly classified programmes leave some trace in records, funding structures, personnel assignments, or oversight mechanisms.

## Why a Crash Claim Needs More Than Witness Memory

AARO did not dismiss testimony outright. In fact, Congress directed the office to gather information from current and former government employees, military personnel, and contractors who claimed direct knowledge of alleged UAP programmes. The office established reporting channels and conducted interviews as part of its historical review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/06/2024-09608/privacy-act-of-1974-system-of-records" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: federalregister.gov">[Federal Register]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">federalregister.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal RegisterPrivacy Act of 1974; System of RecordsMay 6, 2024 — 6 May 2024 — This system of records describes the AARO&#x27;s collection...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 6, 2024</span></span></span>

However, AARO's methodology treated testimony as a lead rather than proof. Investigators attempted to verify claims through independent evidence. The report states that allegations involving specific locations, named individuals, technological tests, and purported reverse-engineering activities were checked against documentary and institutional records. AARO reported finding no empirical evidence supporting claims that government agencies or private companies had been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Report_on_the_Historical_Record_of_U.S._Government_Involvement_with_Unidentified_Anomalous_Phenomena/Volume_1/Section_2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Section 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">more...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important because many modern retrieval allegations involve second-hand or third-hand accounts. AARO's evidentiary standard required corroboration beyond memory. In practical terms, a witness claiming that a secret retrieval programme existed was not enough; investigators sought documentation, physical evidence, programme records, or other independently verifiable indicators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DOPSR 2024 0263 AARO HISTORICAL RECORD REPORT VOLUME</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarAARO Historical Record Report Volume 18 Mar 2024 — found no evidence that U.S. companies ever possessed off-world techno...</span></span></span>

## Records, Materials, Budgets, and Contractor Trails

One of the most distinctive features of AARO's approach was its focus on institutional footprints.

### Programme Records and Oversight

The historical review stated that AARO worked with officials responsible for controlled-access and special-access programme oversight. This was significant because retrieval allegations often claim that programmes were hidden inside highly classified compartments. By consulting officials responsible for managing and tracking such programmes, AARO sought evidence that an unreported retrieval effort had existed outside normal oversight channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/aaro-report-on-the-historical-record-of-u-s-government-involvement-with-unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-uap-volume-i/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[newparadigminstitute.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AAR O Report on the Historical Record of U.S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government ...8 Mar 2024 — This report represents Volume I of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office&#x27;s (AARO) Historical Record Report...</span></span></span>

### Contractor Participation

Many retrieval narratives allege that aerospace companies received recovered materials for analysis. AARO therefore examined claims involving named firms and individuals.

According to the historical report, executives, scientists, and chief technology officers from companies identified by interviewees met with AARO and denied that their organisations had recovered, possessed, or reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology. The office stated that it found no evidence that the companies named in those allegations possessed off-world materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO and denied on the record that they have ever recovered, possessed, or engaged in reverse-engineering of extraterrestrial technology...</span></span></span>

This reflects the office's broader evidence model: if industry had been participating in a long-term exploitation effort, investigators expected contractor records, technical work, facilities, procurement activity, or knowledgeable personnel capable of corroborating the claims.

### Physical Materials

A retrieval programme should also produce artefacts. AARO therefore evaluated claims concerning allegedly recovered materials.

The report describes at least one case involving a metal fragment said to originate from an off-world craft. AARO concluded that the material was ordinary, terrestrial in origin, and lacked extraordinary properties.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/104 May 2024 — AARO has no evidence for the USG reverse-engineering narrat...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

This illustrates the office's preference for examining physical evidence directly whenever such evidence could be obtained rather than relying solely on descriptions of materials.

## The Kona Blue Example

Perhaps the clearest illustration of AARO's evidence test involved a proposed programme known as Kona Blue.

According to the historical report, supporters of Kona Blue believed the government was concealing recovered extraterrestrial technology and sought the creation of a Prospective Special Access Program to investigate and exploit it. The proposal received some consideration but was ultimately rejected. AARO reported that supporters never provided empirical evidence supporting the underlying claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/94 May 2024 — The program was never approved by DHS and its supporters nev...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

The importance of Kona Blue is not that it proved a retrieval programme existed. Rather, it demonstrated how AARO distinguished between belief and evidence. The office found documentation showing that people within government genuinely believed hidden technology programmes existed. What it did not find was evidence demonstrating that the claimed recovered technology actually existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1 2024.pdf/94 May 2024 — The program was never approved by DHS and its supporters nev...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2024</span></span></span>

In effect, Kona Blue became an example of a documented effort motivated by retrieval allegations rather than evidence for the allegations themselves.

## The Core Mechanism of AARO's Evidence Test

The key mechanism behind AARO's evaluation was institutional verification. A crash-retrieval programme, if real, would not simply consist of recovered objects. It would require an enduring support structure involving people, facilities, security systems, budgets, contracts, records, and oversight relationships.

AARO's investigation therefore asked whether those supporting structures could be demonstrated independently of witness narratives. The office reported that after reviewing archives, interviewing witnesses, examining named programmes and companies, consulting oversight officials, and evaluating specific claims and materials, it found no verifiable evidence that such a hidden retrieval architecture existed.
