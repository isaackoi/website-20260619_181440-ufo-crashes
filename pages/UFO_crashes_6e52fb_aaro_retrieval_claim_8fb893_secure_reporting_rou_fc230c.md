## Introduction

Could insider reports reveal hidden UFO programmes? That question was one of the most important tests built into the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) review of historical UFO and crash-retrieval allegations. Rather than relying only on public [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}) or reported sightings, AARO created a secure reporting route specifically for current and former government personnel, military members, and contractors who claimed direct knowledge of alleged [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) anomalous phenomena (UAP) programmes dating back to 1945. The goal was straightforward: collect insider testimony, compare it against classified and unclassified records, and determine whether claims of concealed crash-retrieval or reverse-engineering efforts could be verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/3575027/department-of-defense-launches-secure-reporting-mechanism-on-the-all-domain-ano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: war.gov">[war.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">department of defense launches secure reporting mechanism on the all domain ano</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of WarDepartment of Defense Launches Secure Reporting...31 Oct 2023 — Department of Defense Launches Secure Reporting Me...</span></span></span>

Within the broader debate over [UFO crashes]({{ 'ufo-crashes/' | relative_url }}), this reporting mechanism became a key credibility test. ## Who the Secure Route Was Built For

The reporting mechanism was not designed as a public UFO tip line. From the outset, the Department of Defense restricted it to current or former US government employees, military personnel, and contractor staff with direct knowledge of government UAP-related activities or programmes. Reports were intended to support AARO's congressionally mandated historical review rather than collect general sightings. This distinction mattered because many crash-retrieval allegations rest on claims that information is hidden inside highly classified compartments. AARO therefore prioritised individuals who allegedly worked within, managed, or had authorised access to such activities. The reporting form explicitly sought information about government programmes and activities rather than observations of unusual objects in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Submit-A-Report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[aaro.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Submit A ReportAARO is accepting reports from current or former U.S. Government employees, service members, or contractor personnel w...</span></span></span>

The approach reflected a simple investigative assumption: if a long-running retrieval programme existed, there should be people who encountered it directly during their careers. ## Why Direct Knowledge Mattered to AARO

A recurring problem in UFO crash narratives is the difference between first-hand and second-hand information. Many public allegations involve stories passed through multiple intermediaries, retired officials recounting what others told them, or claims derived from rumours circulating within classified environments.

AARO's reporting system attempted to separate direct access from hearsay. The office repeatedly emphasised the importance of witnesses who personally participated in, managed, or possessed authorised access to programmes under discussion. The reasoning was that first-hand witnesses could provide verifiable details that investigators could test against official records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Submit-A-Report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[aaro.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Submit A ReportAARO is accepting reports from current or former U.S. Government employees, service members, or contractor personnel w...</span></span></span>

This verification process extended beyond interviews. According to AARO's historical review, investigators worked with officials responsible for special-access and controlled-access programmes and were granted access to records needed to examine claims about alleged UAP exploitation, retrieval, or reverse-engineering efforts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/ABC13News/posts/the-department-of-war-released-never-before-seen-files-on-unidentified-anomalous/1397513409076962/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Department of War released “never-before-seen” files ...According to a DOD press release, AARO&#x27;s website will provide photographs and</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">videos of declassified resolved cases, reporting trends, ...Read more</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF)

The office's methodology therefore treated testimony as a starting point rather than an endpoint. An insider claim could generate investigative leads, but the decisive question was whether records, programme histories, funding structures, or authorised personnel lists supported the allegation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/ABC13News/posts/the-department-of-war-released-never-before-seen-files-on-unidentified-anomalous/1397513409076962/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Department of War released “never-before-seen” files ...According to a DOD press release, AARO&#x27;s website will provide photographs and</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">videos of declassified resolved cases, reporting trends, ...Read more</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF)

## How Retrieval Claims Were Tested

The verification process was designed around corroboration.

When witnesses identified a purported programme, organisation, facility, contractor, or historical event, investigators sought records capable of confirming or refuting the claim. In practice, this meant comparing testimony against:

* Classified and unclassified archives.
* Special-access programme oversight records.
* Intelligence-community documentation.
* Department of Defense programme histories.
* Information supplied by officials responsible for programme management and oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/ABC13News/posts/the-department-of-war-released-never-before-seen-files-on-unidentified-anomalous/1397513409076962/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Department of War released “never-before-seen” files ...According to a DOD press release, AARO&#x27;s website will provide photographs and</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">videos of declassified resolved cases, reporting trends, ...Read more</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF)

AARO reported that it investigated numerous named and described programmes alleged to involve recovered non-human technology. Its conclusion was that many of the programmes referenced by witnesses were real national-security activities, but investigators found no [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) that these programmes were engaged in recovering, storing, or reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/ABC13News/posts/the-department-of-war-released-never-before-seen-files-on-unidentified-anomalous/1397513409076962/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Department of War released “never-before-seen” files ...According to a DOD press release, AARO&#x27;s website will provide photographs and</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">videos of declassified resolved cases, reporting trends, ...Read more</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2024/Mar/08/2003409233/-1/-1/0/DOPSR-2024-0263-AARO-HISTORICAL-RECORD-REPORT-VOLUME-1-2024.PDF)
