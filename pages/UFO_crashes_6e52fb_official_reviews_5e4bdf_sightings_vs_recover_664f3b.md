## Introduction

[Official reviews]({{ 'official-reviews/' | relative_url }}) of UFO or UAP reports consistently draw a sharp distinction between an unexplained sighting and a claimed crash recovery. The reason is straightforward: the two claims require very different kinds of evidence. A witness report, radar track, photograph, or video may remain unidentified because the available information is incomplete. A crash-retrieval claim, by contrast, implies the existence of physical objects, recovery operations, transport records, laboratory examinations, personnel involvement, and a documented [chain of custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}). Official investigations therefore treat “we do not know what was seen” as a separate question from “a government recovered alien technology or bodies.” <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — • No evidence of extraterrestrial origin of UFO/UAP were discovered.... alien spacecraft...</span></span></span>

## What an Unresolved Sighting Can and Cannot Prove

An unresolved sighting demonstrates only that investigators could not confidently identify the reported object or event using the available data. It does not, by itself, establish what the object was.

This principle has appeared repeatedly in official UFO investigations. Project Blue Book closed with hundreds of cases still classified as unidentified, yet the Air Force stated that these unresolved reports did not constitute evidence of extraterrestrial vehicles or technology beyond known science. The category “unidentified” reflected a lack of sufficient information, not a positive conclusion about origin. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives+2U.S. Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">no evidence indicating that sightings categorized as &quot;unidentified&quot; are extraterrestrial vehicles.... Modern reviews apply the same logic. The Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office ([AARO]({{ 'aaro/' | relative_url }})) has stated that some reports remain unresolved because of limited or poor-quality data, sensor limitations, incomplete observations, or missing contextual information. An unresolved case is therefore an evidential gap rather than proof of an extraordinary explanation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO HomeHas the Department found any evidence of extraterrestrial technology? ## Why Crash Claims Require Physical Records

A crash-retrieval allegation is fundamentally different because it concerns events that should leave tangible traces.

If an [aircraft]({{ 'aircraft/' | relative_url }}), spacecraft, or unknown vehicle crashes and is recovered, investigators would expect evidence such as:

* Recovery reports and operational orders.
* Transportation and storage [records]({{ 'records/' | relative_url }}).

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Material samples available for testing.
* Laboratory analyses and technical assessments.
* Personnel testimony linked to specific documented events.
* Budgetary, contractual, or programme records.
* A chain of custody showing where recovered items went and who handled them.

</div>

Unlike a fleeting sighting, a crash recovery is not a momentary observation. It is an extended logistical process involving multiple people, locations, and documents. Because of this, official reviews seek corroborating records rather than relying solely on witness recollections. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2The Washington Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — • No evidence of extraterrestrial origin of UFO/UAP were discovered.... alien spacecraft...</span></span></span>

This evidential expectation explains why investigators often separate testimony about strange objects from testimony about recovered hardware. The latter can, in principle, be independently verified through records and physical artefacts. The former may not be verifiable at all if the event has already passed and little data were collected. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — • No evidence of extraterrestrial origin of UFO/UAP were discovered.... alien spacecraft...</span></span></span>

## How Official Reviews Apply the Evidence Threshold

Official reviews generally use a higher evidential threshold for crash claims because the claim itself is stronger.

A report that a pilot observed an unusual object requires evidence that an observation occurred. A claim that a government secretly recovered alien technology requires evidence for an entire sequence of events: crash, recovery, transport, storage, analysis, and concealment. Each step creates additional factual assertions that can potentially be checked. The more extensive the claim, the more extensive the expected evidence. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — • No evidence of extraterrestrial origin of UFO/UAP were discovered.... alien spacecraft...</span></span></span>

This approach is visible in AARO's 2024 historical review. The office examined longstanding allegations involving recovered spacecraft, reverse-engineering programmes, contractor involvement, and hidden repositories of extraterrestrial technology. The report stated that investigators found no verifiable evidence supporting claims that the US government or private industry possessed recovered extraterrestrial technology or operated secret reverse-engineering programmes. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol 1 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — • No evidence of extraterrestrial origin of UFO/UAP were discovered.... alien spacecraft...</span></span></span>

Notably, this conclusion was presented separately from the question of whether every UAP sighting has been explained. The review acknowledged continuing investigations into unidentified reports while finding no evidence for recovered alien hardware. The two questions were treated as analytically distinct. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO+2AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO HomeHas the Department found any evidence of extraterrestrial technology? ## Why Witness Testimony Alone Is Usually Not Enough

Witness testimony can be valuable, particularly when multiple observers independently describe the same event. However, crash-[retrieval claims]({{ 'retrieval-claims/' | relative_url }}) generally require more than testimony because they concern physical objects that should leave objective traces.

Official reviews often distinguish between first-hand observation and second-hand reporting. A person may sincerely report what they believe they were told about a recovery programme without possessing direct access to the alleged artefacts or records. ## The Roswell Example Illustrates the Difference

Roswell remains the clearest illustration of why official reviews separate sightings from crash recoveries.
